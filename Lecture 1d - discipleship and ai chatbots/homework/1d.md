## 1. "Things As They Really Are"

I think the most important thing to remember as an agent engineer, especially in light of Elder Bednar's talk, is AI will never become God and that our AI agents should not take away from the agency of children of God. Our goal in developing AI agents should be for the betterment of society and further development of humans. In our efforts to create, we should never be striving for something that will replace God or the Holy Ghost in people's lives nor treat users like objects to be acted upon. 

I think so many AI products are used to distract, captivate, and entrap users' time and attention to maximize profit. As disciples of Jesus Christ who are agent engineers, we must make sure the products we make with AI respect users enough to not become addictive or huge dopamine hits.

That being said, AI models are powerful and can be used in a variety of righteous ways. So in addition to not using our skills in agentic engineering in morally desensitizing ways, we should use them to further God's work and promote further growth of God's children.

## 2. "Microsoft retreats from personal AI chatbot race, refocusing Copilot on the workplace"

Microsoft, up until this announcement, has been trying to compete with other companies' personal AI companion chatbots, including OpenAI's ChatGPT and Google's Gemini. Due to the lack of success the personal Copilot experienced and general desire of users for Copilot to be more integrated in Microsoft's massive library of applications and software, Microsoft just announced that it is merging its 2 versions of Copilot, the workplace version and the home one, into one unified product. This Copilot will focus mainly on being effective in helping users accomplish tasks rather than being a personable companion like ChatGPT. 

```
“We’re not going to build a Copilot that’s like your personal companion,” Lamanna said. “That’s just not what people want from Microsoft. We help you get stuff done.”
```

Now with the single unified app or website, users can have Copilot interact with and edit Word and Excel documents. Additionally, with the coding tab, users can set up dashboards and automated tasks. There is still also the assistant Microsoft Scout that is now Autopilot that will be a part of the unified Copilot product too.

And interestingly enough, certain anthropomorphic features of Copilot, such as an animated blob of an avatar that popped up during conversations, will no longer be a part of the chat, really just driving the point home that Copilot is now about getting work done.

## 3. Appropriate Use of AI Chatbots Discussion

Human users should never develop a synthetic emotional connection with a Chatbot. Any sort of friendship or romantic relationship with a Chatbot is very inappropriate, as it warps a person's ability to distinguish real relationships from fake, thus causing harm in actual human connections that user has or would try to create. Additionally, when a person turns first to a chatbot for relationship advice or to be validated in an issue in their relationship without going to talk through the issue with the other human involved, that also deteriorates the foundation of that relationship. This is especially serious in a marriage. If a spouse turns to an AI for emotional connection or validation before turning to their spouse, I believe that is a betrayal of fidelity within a marriage.

In short, the use of AI for any sort of emotional or relationship connection is not only inappropriate but could be considered ethically and morally wrong.

That said, the use of chatbots for the study and learning of information and skills is appropriate and should be encouraged, since to me that's their greatest purpose. Chats are a tool for users to learn and grow in skills and abilities and critical thinking, for the betterment of that person and humanity. So any use of a chatbot to actually promote learning and growth is appropriate.

Additionally, I'll admit that chats can be used recreationally for fun. Generating funny stories, jokes, scenarios, images (as long as these things don't cause harm to others) seem to be an appropriate use of them. But this fun must never lead into any sort of emotional dependence or connection as I have already mentioned.
