## Title

In Appreciation of Friction: Embracing the Awkward

## Speaker

C. Shane Reese, President of Brigham Young University

## Key Takeaways

- BYU is intended to help students become the people God wants them to become and progress on their journeys back to their heavenly home.
- Students should embrace awkwardness, discomfort, productive struggle, and failure because these experiences are signs and instruments of growth.
- Students should seek help from others, be patient with themselves, and recognize that learning and becoming excellent take time.
- Students should resist excessive convenience and technological frictionlessness by thinking for themselves, developing discernment, and avoiding cognitive and moral dependence on algorithms.
- Students should put away earbuds and phones often so they can engage in meaningful human interactions and serve others.
- Small acts of courage and conversation can lead to lasting relationships, spiritual growth, and divinely meaningful outcomes.
- Students should follow Jesus Christ’s example by noticing, listening to, and ministering individually to people around them.
- Worship in the temple should be a priority, especially during the new school year and in anticipation of the newly dedicated Salt Lake Temple.
- BYU’s dress and grooming standards, as well as other forms of constructive discipline, can help students unite in common aspirations and prepare for future service.
- Students should turn to Jesus Christ, whose Atonement can consecrate their struggles, wipe away their wrongs, and make the injustices of life right.

## Speaker Emphasis

- “Embrace the awkward” is the central counsel of the address and is applied to social interactions, learning, missionary work, leadership, and personal growth.
- Awkwardness and productive struggle are not defects to be avoided; they are “badges of becoming” and places where people learn, develop, and change.
- The speaker repeatedly urges students to engage with other people directly: remove earbuds, put away phones, wave, smile, start conversations, and notice those who may be alone or on the periphery.
- The speaker emphasizes the danger of an increasingly “frictionless” life, especially when technology causes people to stop thinking, discerning, or developing character for themselves.
- Jesus Christ’s outward-focused attention to individuals is presented as the model for how students should live and minister.
- The speaker repeatedly connects BYU’s campus life, traditions, standards, worship, and relationships with preparation for service in the home, professions, Church, and communities.
- The speaker personally stresses gratitude for the patience others have shown him and describes learning to love—and sometimes survive—awkward moments.
- The address repeatedly returns to the idea that Christ will support students through difficult and uncomfortable experiences and consecrate those experiences for their good.
