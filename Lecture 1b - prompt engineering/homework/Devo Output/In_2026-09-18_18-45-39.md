## Title

In Appreciation of Friction: Embracing the Awkward

## Speaker

C. Shane Reese

## Key Takeaways

- Embrace awkwardness and productive friction; they signal growth, develop discernment, and are essential to learning.
- Prioritize worship in the house of the Lord—especially in this season leading to the Salt Lake Temple dedication—so Christ can change and sustain you.
- Choose human connection over frictionless convenience: remove earbuds, put your phone away, talk to people, smile, and wave.
- Use technology and AI wisely; avoid letting convenience lead to cognitive offloading or moral atrophy; cultivate faith, character, intellect, and work ethic.
- Follow prophetic counsel: strengthen faith in Jesus Christ, increase humility, seek help from others, and be patient in the process.
- Live BYU’s dress and grooming standards and share your talents openly to shine Christ’s light and bless others.
- Look outward and minister like Jesus—see people individually, listen to God more than to devices, and trust Christ’s Atonement to consecrate your efforts and brighten your future.

## Speaker Emphasis

- “Embrace the awkward” as a personal mantra; awkward moments are “badges of becoming.”
- Friction as a teacher: “Friction is where discernment lives. It’s where thinking starts.”
- Deliberately disconnecting: “remove your earbuds” and “bury your phone” to make room for real human engagement.
- Heightened, campus-wide emphasis on temple worship tied to the Salt Lake Temple rededication.
- BYU as a loving family with rich traditions; students as President Kimball’s prophesied “brilliant stars.”
- Framing this moment as the opening of “the second half of BYU’s second century.”
- Simple daily ministries: waving, smiling, starting conversations; learning to love through awkward moments.
- Becoming a new creature in Christ at BYU—uniting study and faith under discipleship.
