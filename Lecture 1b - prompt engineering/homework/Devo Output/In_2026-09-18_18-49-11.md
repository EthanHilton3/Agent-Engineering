## Title

In Appreciation of Friction: Embracing the Awkward

## Speaker

C. Shane Reese, President of Brigham Young University

## Key Takeaways

- BYU exists to help students become the people God wants them to become and progress on their journeys back to their heavenly home.
- Students should worship in the house of the Lord, draw closer to Jesus Christ, and make temple worship a priority.
- Awkwardness, productive struggle, and failure are normal and valuable parts of growth; students should “embrace the awkward” rather than avoid discomfort.
- Students should resist the risks of increasingly frictionless lives, including cognitive offloading and dependence on technology to think or act for them.
- Students should put away earbuds and phones, engage in face-to-face conversations, and seek meaningful human interaction, especially with people who may be isolated or overlooked.
- Jesus Christ is the model for focusing upward and outward, seeing people individually, ministering to those on the periphery, and engaging in uncomfortable but meaningful interactions.
- BYU’s standards, including dress and grooming standards, can unite the campus community and bless students’ futures.
- Small acts of courage, friendliness, and engagement can lead to lifelong relationships, service, spiritual growth, and divine opportunities.
- Christ’s Atonement can consecrate awkwardness, mistakes, and suffering for students’ good, wipe away wrongs, and make injustices right.

## Speaker Emphasis

- “Embrace the awkward” is the central counsel of the address and is repeatedly connected with growth, courage, learning, service, and relationships.
- Awkwardness is presented as “a sign of growth,” while productive struggle and failure are described as “badges of becoming.”
- The speaker repeatedly urges students to find friction rather than eliminate every inconvenience or discomfort.
- He strongly emphasizes putting down phones, removing earbuds, and engaging directly with other people: “Embrace the human. Say something to one of God’s children.”
- The speaker returns repeatedly to the contrast between technology’s inward, downward focus and Christ’s “upward and outward” attention to individuals.
- He stresses that Christ should be the example for how students respond to discomfort, minister to others, and live abundantly.
- The speaker repeatedly frames BYU as a family and emphasizes belonging, shared traditions, unity in Christ, and serving together.
- He personally emphasizes that patience from others helped him through awkward experiences and that he has learned both to love and to learn from those moments.
