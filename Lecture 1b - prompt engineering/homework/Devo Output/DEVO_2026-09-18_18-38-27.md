## Title

In Appreciation of Friction: Embracing the Awkward

## Speaker

C. Shane Reese, President of Brigham Young University

## Key Takeaways

- BYU is intended to help students become the people God wants them to become and return to their heavenly home.
- Students should worship in the house of the Lord and make temple worship a priority during the demands of a new school year.
- Awkwardness, productive struggle, mistakes, and failure are necessary parts of growth and should be embraced rather than avoided.
- Students should seek help from others, develop patience, and move beyond their comfort zones.
- An increasingly frictionless life, especially one shaped by technology and artificial intelligence, can weaken discernment, independent thinking, character, and social skills.
- Students should put away earbuds and phones, engage in meaningful human interactions, and look for opportunities to speak with and serve others.
- Jesus Christ is the model for focusing outward, noticing individuals, ministering personally, and engaging in uncomfortable but important conversations.
- BYU’s standards and shared practices can unite students in their common pursuit of learning by study and faith.
- Students should trust that Jesus Christ’s Atonement can consecrate their struggles for their good, wipe away their wrongs, and make the injustices of life right.

## Speaker Emphasis

- “Embrace the awkward” is presented as the central advice for the semester.
- The speaker repeatedly connects awkwardness, discomfort, friction, struggle, and failure with growth and becoming.
- The speaker stresses that students should engage with other people directly instead of retreating into phones, earbuds, or frictionless technology.
- The speaker repeatedly calls attention to Jesus Christ’s example of looking upward and outward, seeing people deeply, and ministering to individuals.
- The speaker emphasizes that BYU is a family and a community seeking unity in Christ.
- The speaker returns to the importance of patience, seeking help, and learning through difficult experiences.
- The speaker personally emphasizes that awkward moments have taught him to love and learn, even when they have sometimes merely helped him survive.
- The speaker repeatedly urges students to develop habits that will prepare them for future service in their homes, professions, the Church, and communities.
