## Title

In Appreciation of Friction: Embracing the Awkward

## Speaker

C. Shane Reese

## Key Takeaways

- BYU is intended to help students become the people God wants them to become and progress on their journeys back to their heavenly home.
- Students should worship in the house of the Lord and make temple worship a priority during the school year.
- Awkwardness, productive struggle, and failure are natural parts of growth; students should not be embarrassed by their stumbles but should regard them as “badges of becoming.”
- Students should resist an increasingly frictionless, technology-mediated life when it replaces independent thinking, discernment, character development, or meaningful human interaction.
- Students should remove distractions such as earbuds and phones, initiate conversations, and engage with people around them, because human connection can lead to growth, service, fellowship, and unexpected blessings.
- Students should follow Jesus Christ’s example of looking upward and outward, seeing people deeply, ministering individually, and being willing to have uncomfortable conversations.
- BYU’s dress and grooming standards, along with other forms of disciplined effort, can help students unite in common aspirations and prepare for future service in their homes, professions, Church, and communities.
- Students should trust in Jesus Christ and His Atonement, which can consecrate difficult experiences for their good, wipe away wrongs, and make the injustices of life right.

## Speaker Emphasis

- “Embrace the awkward” is the central repeated counsel of the address, applied to new relationships, failure, service, learning, and uncomfortable conversations.
- Productive struggle and friction are valuable rather than merely inconvenient; the speaker repeatedly connects them with discernment, thinking, growth, and becoming.
- The speaker repeatedly urges students to “embrace the human” by removing earbuds, putting away phones, speaking to strangers, smiling, waving, and noticing people who may be on the periphery.
- Turning toward Jesus Christ is presented as the ultimate model: Christ’s gaze was “upward and outward, not inward and downward,” and He ministered through direct, individual interaction.
- The speaker stresses that awkward experiences can teach, change, and prepare people for future service, drawing on his own freshman experience, presidential experiences, and personal examples.
- The speaker repeatedly frames BYU as a family and a spiritual community united in learning, worship, service, and a shared journey toward God.
- The speaker emphasizes the importance of temple worship, especially in anticipation of the newly dedicated Salt Lake Temple and the beginning of BYU’s second half of its second century.
