D. Todd Christofferson (Second Counselor in the First Presidency)
O Be Wise
Today I feel a bit like the Book of Mormon prophet Jacob, when he felt compelled by the Spirit to address subjects that were not particularly edifying, having to use “boldness of speech,” as he put it, instead of feasting on “the pleasing word of God.” So, with a sense of duty, I turn to the subject of gambling.
President Gordon B. Hinckley, in addressing this subject over 20 years ago, mentioned a story about former United States President Calvin Coolidge, known as a man of few words. As Coolidge returned home from church one Sunday, his wife asked him what the preacher spoke about. Hereplied, “Sin.”
“Well, what did he say?” she asked.
“He was against it,” replied Coolidge.
President Hinckley said, “I think I could answer the question concerning gambling just that briefly. We are against it.”
Putting money or something of value at risk to bet on a particular outcome or event is an activity that has plagued mankind almost, I suppose, from the beginning of time. And time has not made it any more worthwhile or any less harmful. Unfortunately, in recent years, gambling has become incredibly pervasive.




(0.46) D. Todd Christofferson (Second Counselor in the First Presidency)
O Be Wise
------------------------------
President Oaks clearly stated the moral case against gambling some years ago in these words: “What does gambling do to its participants? The attitude of taking something from someone else in order to enhance our own position—[which is] the essence of gambling—leads us away from the giving path of Christ and toward the taking path of the adversary. The act of taking or trying to take something from someone else without giving value in return is destructive of spiritual sensitivities.”
Today, I am pleading with all of us to live life on a higher plane. God has granted life and agency to each of us, and each of us is accountable to Him for how we live that life. A key part of a life well-lived is contributing to the well-being of others. It is being productive in worthy endeavors. It is being and doing better without others doing worse. It comes by supporting and rewarding work rather than idle pursuits. Work leads to prosperous, just, and equitable societies. Gambling, even for fun, leads in the opposite direction.

“And through thy [conduct] shall the weak brother perish, for whom Christ died?”
In the context of my message today, Paul is saying, “Since your recreational gambling may lead a weaker brother or sister into temptation, just don’t do it. Every soul is precious, and Christ died for him, for her, as He did for you.”

Trusting the Lord Through Tithes and Offerings
------------------------------
When we began teaching the law of tithing and the promised blessings that come to those who obey it, with great sadness she said to me, “Elder Giuffra, I clean houses and wash clothes for several families. The next morning, I called my mission president, President Fernando Caballero, to seek his counsel. In my mind, I wondered whether the Lordmight have an exception for someone in her circumstances.
President Caballero read to me the account of the widow’s mite and then said, “Elder Giuffra, you are no one to deny this sister the blessings of the law of tithing. There are no exceptions to this commandment.”
