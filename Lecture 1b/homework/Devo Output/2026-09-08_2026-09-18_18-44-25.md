## Title

In Appreciation of Friction: Embracing the Awkward

## Speaker

C. Shane Reese

## Key Takeaways

- BYU is meant to help students on their journey back to their heavenly home and to become the person God wants them to become.
- Students should make worship in the temple a priority and draw closer to Jesus Christ, especially during challenging moments.
- Growth usually involves awkwardness, productive struggle, failure, and discomfort; students should not be embarrassed by those experiences but see them as part of becoming.
- A frictionless, technology-shaped life can weaken discernment, thinking, and human connection if people let devices and algorithms do too much for them.
- Students should choose real human interaction over retreating into phones and earbuds, because those awkward conversations can bless lives and open meaningful opportunities.
- Jesus Christ is the model for outward-focused, personal ministering; students should look up, notice others, and serve individuals in simple daily settings.
- BYU standards and community practices, even when uncomfortable, can shape students for future service in their homes, professions, the Church, and their communities.
- Christ’s Atonement can consecrate both awkwardness and suffering for good, wipe away wrongs, and make injustices right.

## Speaker Emphasis

- The repeated counsel to 'embrace the awkward.'
- Friction as valuable: productive struggle, discomfort, and even failure as places where growth and discernment happen.
- Concern about 'frictionless' living through technology, especially AI, phones, and earbuds, and the need to resist letting them replace thinking and human connection.
- Intentional face-to-face engagement on campus: talk to people, wave, smile, and notice those around you.
- Temple worship and drawing closer to Jesus Christ through the house of the Lord.
- Jesus Christ’s outward, individual ministering as the pattern students should follow.
- BYU as a loving family and as a place designed to help students become who God wants them to become.
