## Title

In Appreciation of Friction: Embracing the Awkward

## Speaker

C. Shane Reese, President of Brigham Young University

## Key Takeaways

- BYU’s purpose is to help students progress toward their “journeys back to our heavenly home” and become the person “God wants you to become.”
- Make worship in the temple a priority; drawing closer to Jesus Christ through temple worship will change and support you, including during challenging moments.
- “Embrace the awkward”: awkwardness and discomfort often signal growth; productive struggle, stumbles, and even failure are part of learning and becoming.
- Friction has value; an overly “frictionless” life (including frictionless tech and AI-enabled convenience) can diminish discernment, independent thinking, and moral/intellectual development.
- Take practical steps to reintroduce healthy friction and human connection: remove earbuds on campus, put the phone away, and initiate conversations and fellowship.
- Avoid “cognitive offloading” (letting algorithms do the thinking); personal effort is necessary for developing intellect, character, and social skills.
- Live discipleship by turning from screens to people and to Christ; meaningful ministering is often inconvenient and requires attention “upward and outward.”
- BYU standards (including dress and grooming) can feel uncomfortable but are framed as unifying and blessing students, helping them stand out and pursue “learning…by study and also by faith.”
- Jesus Christ is the model for individual, attentive ministry; follow Him by staying longer with those in need, smiling more, and engaging others.
- Hope and promise: students’ futures are “bright”; Christ’s Atonement can “consecrate it all for your good,” wipe wrongs clean, and make injustices right.

## Speaker Emphasis

- “Embrace the awkward.”
- The value of “friction” (and warning against “frictionless” living).
- Concrete behavioral counsel: “remove your earbuds” and “bury your phone deep in your backpack.”
- Turning away from screens toward real people and toward Jesus Christ (gaze “upward and outward”).
- Temple worship as a campus emphasis tied to the (re)dedication of the Salt Lake Temple.
- Growth through patience and process (including patience with oneself and others).
- The idea that avoiding discomfort undermines development (muscle/intellect/character analogies; “Taking up your cross often requires first putting down your phone!”).
