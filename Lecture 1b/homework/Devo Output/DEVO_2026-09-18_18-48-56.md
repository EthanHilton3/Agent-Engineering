## Title

In Appreciation of Friction: Embracing the Awkward

## Speaker

C. Shane Reese, President of Brigham Young University

## Key Takeaways

- BYU is intended to help students become the people God wants them to become and progress on their journeys back to their heavenly home.
- Students should embrace awkwardness, discomfort, productive struggle, and failure as necessary parts of growth and becoming.
- Students should seek help from others, be patient with themselves, and move beyond their comfort zones.
- Worship in the house of the Lord should be a priority, especially during challenging and busy seasons.
- Students should resist excessive reliance on technology, earbuds, phones, and artificial intelligence when these prevent human interaction, independent thinking, intellectual development, or moral growth.
- Friction can foster discernment, thinking, character, service, and meaningful relationships; students should intentionally seek appropriate opportunities for human interaction.
- Following Jesus Christ means looking outward, noticing individuals, ministering personally, and engaging in uncomfortable but valuable conversations.
- Students should live BYU’s dress and grooming standards and engage with others through simple acts such as waving, smiling, listening, and starting conversations.
- Jesus Christ’s Atonement can consecrate life’s awkwardness and difficulties for good, wipe away wrongs, and make injustices right.

- The promise that Christ’s Atonement can redeem and consecrate uncomfortable experiences for students’ good.
