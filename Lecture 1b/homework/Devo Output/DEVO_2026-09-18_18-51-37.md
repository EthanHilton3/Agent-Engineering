## Title

In Appreciation of Friction: Embracing the Awkward

## Speaker

C. Shane Reese

## Key Takeaways

- BYU is intended to help students succeed on their journeys back to their heavenly home and become the people God wants them to become; students should see themselves as part of a BYU family united in Christ.
- Students should prioritize worship in the house of the Lord, especially during this historic semester, because Jesus Christ can change and support them through difficult moments.
- Awkwardness, discomfort, productive struggle, stumbles, and failure are necessary parts of growth. Students should be patient with themselves and seek help from others.
- Students should be cautious about increasingly frictionless, AI-mediated lives and preserve their own thinking, discernment, character, work ethic, and social skills.
- Students should remove earbuds and put away phones so they can initiate conversations and fellowship. Ordinary interactions can lead to meaningful service and life-changing relationships.
- Jesus Christ is the model for individual ministering: He looked upward and outward, noticed people deeply, and served those who were often on the periphery.
- Students should openly use their talents to glorify God, live BYU’s dress and grooming standards, and practice simple engagement—smiling, waving, and speaking with others—as preparation for future service.
- Students can trust Christ’s Atonement to consecrate difficult experiences for their good, wipe clean their wrongs, and make the injustices of life right.

## Speaker Emphasis

- “Embrace the awkward” serves as the address’s organizing refrain. Reese presents discomfort as something to accept in learning, relationships, service, and discipleship.
- He repeatedly reframes friction as productive rather than defective, emphasizing that struggle and failure are “badges of becoming” and that “friction is where discernment lives. It’s where thinking starts.”
- He returns repeatedly to concrete alternatives to screen-centered living: remove earbuds, bury the phone, talk with people, smile, wave, and “listen to God more and your earbuds less.”
- The phrase “embrace the human” captures his repeated call to choose conversation, fellowship, and personal presence over convenient digital distraction.
- He places Jesus Christ’s upward-and-outward gaze and individualized ministry at the center of the address’s model for human interaction.
- He stresses belonging and institutional purpose by repeatedly describing BYU as a family and recalling the university’s historic position in the second half of its second century.
- He gives special weight to worship in the house of the Lord as a priority for the semester.
- Through self-deprecating stories about his own awkward experiences, he personally models humility, patience, and learning from uncomfortable moments.
