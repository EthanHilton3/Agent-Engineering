## Title

In Appreciation of Friction: Embracing the Awkward

## Speaker

C. Shane Reese

## Key Takeaways

- BYU’s stated purpose is to help students return to their heavenly home and become the people God wants them to become through learning, worship, service, and unity in Christ.
- Make worship in the house of the Lord a priority. Reese teaches that temple worship draws disciples closer to Jesus Christ, changes them, and supports them through difficult moments.
- Embrace awkwardness and discomfort rather than treating them as failure. Asking for help, practicing patiently, struggling productively, and stumbling are necessary parts of growth and excellence.
- Do not allow the pursuit of frictionless convenience—especially through artificial intelligence and algorithms—to replace discernment, independent thought, effort, character development, or social skills.
- Remove earbuds and put away phones often enough to talk, smile, wave, notice others, and serve. Small acts of social courage can lead to significant relationships and prepare students for future service.
- Live BYU’s dress and grooming standards even when they feel uncomfortable; Reese says they can bless students and unite the community in shared aspirations of learning by study and faith.
- Follow Christ’s outward-looking, individual ministry by spending time with people in need, engaging those on the periphery, and making room for care that may be inconvenient.
- Turn to Jesus Christ and trust His Atonement to consecrate awkward and uncomfortable experiences, cleanse wrongs, and ultimately make life’s injustices right.
